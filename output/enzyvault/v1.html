The challenge

Why conventional storage falls short

The world is generating more data than existing storage infrastructure can sustainably preserve. Hard drives, magnetic tape, and cloud systems require energy, refresh cycles, and physical space. DNA offers extraordinary density — but practical DNA storage still faces major hurdles.

DNA degrades over time

Without stabilization, DNA molecules break down under environmental stress, compromising long-term data integrity.

Cold storage is expensive

Most DNA storage requires continuous refrigeration and specialized infrastructure, adding significant operational cost.

Retrieval must stay accurate

Molecular storage is only valuable if encoded information can be recovered reliably at high fidelity across timescales.

Preservation needs new methods

Long-term preservation at scale requires fundamentally new molecular stabilization approaches beyond existing techniques.

Our platform

Enzymatic DNA stabilization

Our proprietary enzyme platform is being developed to promote controlled DNA intertwining, creating higher-order molecular structures designed to resist environmental degradation. The result is a new approach to DNA preservation: not just storing DNA, but structurally reinforcing it.

  • Structural reinforcement

    Controlled higher-order molecular organization protects DNA from environmental degradation without sacrificing retrievability.

  • RNA-guided scaffolds

    Optional programmable hybrid DNA/RNA architectures for advanced stabilization configurations.

  • Sequencing compatibility

    Designed for compatibility with standard NGS and long-read sequencing workflows for reliable, scalable retrieval.

How it works

From digital data to stable molecular archive in five key stages.

2

3

4

5

Retrieve

Stored information recovered using sequencing-based readout workflows compatible with existing infrastructure.

Impact

Why it matters

DNA is one of nature's most powerful information storage media — dense, durable, and biologically universal. To become practical infrastructure, it must move beyond fragile molecules and specialized cold-chain environments.

Lower storage energy costs

Reduce reliance on refrigeration and active cooling, lowering operational costs for long-term archives.

Longer archival timelines

Support durable preservation for scientific, cultural, medical, and institutional records beyond current media lifespans.

Higher molecular density

Improve physical compaction through controlled higher-order DNA structures, enabling more data in less space.

Future-compatible retrieval

Maintain compatibility with sequencing-based readout technologies as they continue to improve and reduce in cost.
